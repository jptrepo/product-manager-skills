## Purpose
Conduct systematic vendor research for higher education IT procurements using specialized higher-ed resources (Educause, Internet2, peer institutions) and vendor competitive analysis. Use this to validate vendor claims, identify alternatives, understand peer experiences, and make informed procurement decisions grounded in the higher education community.

This is not a general vendor evaluation—it's a higher-ed-specific research process that leverages academic institutional networks and specialized resources unavailable to commercial buyers.

## Key Concepts

### Why Higher Ed Vendor Research is Different

**Unique Higher Ed Requirements:**
- **FERPA compliance** (not just GDPR)
- **Academic calendar constraints** (summer implementations, semester deadlines)
- **Shared governance** (faculty buy-in matters)
- **Consortia purchasing** (Internet2 NET+, regional buying groups)
- **Open-source preferences** (community-supported tools)
- **Research use cases** (not just administrative)

**Higher Ed-Specific Risks:**
- Vendor optimized for K-12 or corporate (different user models)
- No BAA or institutional data protection
- Poor integration with common higher-ed systems (Canvas, Banner, PeopleSoft, Workday)
- Pricing models incompatible with higher-ed budgets
- Limited understanding of academic workflows

### Three-Tier Research Approach

**Tier 1: Higher Ed Community Research** (Start here)
- Educause resources and case studies
- Internet2 NET+ trusted services
- Peer institution reference calls

**Tier 2: Vendor Direct Research**
- Vendor website and documentation
- Product demos and trials
- Higher-ed client references (vendor-provided)

**Tier 3: Competitive Analysis**
- Alternative vendors serving higher ed
- Build vs. buy vs. open-source options
- Total cost of ownership comparison

### Anti-Patterns (What This Is NOT)
- **Not vendor marketing research:** Don't rely solely on vendor claims
- **Not generic software evaluation:** Use higher-ed-specific criteria
- **Not rushed due diligence:** Allow 2-4 weeks for thorough research
- **Not isolated decision:** Engage peer institutions early

### When to Use This
- Evaluating new software vendor for procurement
- Vetting AI tools for AI committee review
- Comparing alternatives during RFP process
- Validating vendor claims before contract negotiation
- Annual review of existing vendors

### When NOT to Use This
- Renewing existing vendor with satisfactory performance
- Emergency procurement (security incident)
- Open-source tools with community support
- Free tools with minimal risk

## Application

### Step 1: Educause Research (30-60 minutes)

**Educause Resources to Check:**

1. **Educause Review and Research Publications**
   - Search: educause.edu/review
   - Look for: Case studies, vendor comparisons, implementation lessons
   - Example: "Canvas vs. Blackboard migration experiences"

2. **Educause Community Groups**
   - Search: educause.edu/community
   - Find groups: CIOs, Security Professionals, Teaching & Learning, etc.
   - Post questions to relevant community (if member)

3. **Educause Annual Conference Proceedings**
   - Search: educause.edu/annual-conference
   - Filter by: Vendor name, product category, use case
   - Look for: Session recordings, presentation slides, peer experiences

4. **Educause AI Landscape** (for AI tools)
   - Search: educause.edu/AI
   - Review: AI tools matrix, governance frameworks, bias resources

**What to Capture:**
- Which institutions are using this vendor?
- What use cases (academic, administrative, research)?
- What challenges did they encounter?
- What alternatives were considered?
- Any implementation timelines or costs mentioned?

**Red Flags:**
- No Educause mentions (vendor unknown in higher ed)
- Negative case studies or cautionary tales
- Vendor mentioned but minimal adoption

---

### Step 2: Internet2 Community Research (30-60 minutes)

**Internet2 Resources:**

1. **Internet2 NET+ Services**
   - URL: internet2.edu/netplus
   - Check if vendor is NET+ trusted service
   - Review: Pricing, terms, peer usage data
   - **Benefit:** Pre-negotiated contracts, consortium pricing, vetted security

2. **Internet2 Community Calls & Forums**
   - Access via institutional membership
   - Search: Listservs, community calls, archived discussions
   - Ask: "Has anyone used [vendor] for [use case]?"

3. **Internet2 InCommon (for identity/SSO)**
   - Check: Does vendor support InCommon federation?
   - Integration: SAML/SSO for seamless campus authentication

**What to Capture:**
- Is this a NET+ service? (Pre-vetted, consortium pricing)
- How many Internet2 members use this?
- Any community-reported issues?
- InCommon/SSO support?

**Green Flags:**
- NET+ trusted service (highest confidence)
- Active user community among Internet2 members
- InCommon federation support

---

### Step 3: Peer Institution Research (1-2 weeks)

**Identify 3-5 peer institutions using this vendor:**

**Sources:**
- Educause case studies (institutional names)
- Vendor-provided reference list (verify independently)
- LinkedIn search: "[Institution] + [Vendor]" (find users)
- Conference attendees (Educause, regional CIO forums)

**Selection Criteria for Peers:**
- Similar size (enrollment, budget)
- Similar Carnegie classification (R1, R2, teaching-focused, etc.)
- Similar IT maturity (not comparing small college to MIT)
- Regional proximity (if relevant for support)

**Reference Call Structure:**

**Preparation:**
- Email introduction explaining your evaluation
- Offer to reciprocate (share your experiences with their inquiries)
- Schedule 30-minute call

**Key Questions:**

1. **Implementation Experience**
   - How long did implementation take?
   - What challenges did you encounter?
   - What would you do differently?
   - Timeline: RFP → contract → go-live?

2. **User Adoption**
   - How did faculty/staff/students respond?
   - What training was required?
   - Any user resistance or complaints?

3. **Vendor Support**
   - How responsive is vendor support?
   - Have you had any major incidents? How were they handled?
   - Do they understand higher-ed needs?

4. **Integration**
   - What systems does this integrate with?
   - Any integration challenges (SIS, LMS, SSO)?
   - Did vendor support integration, or DIY?

5. **Cost Reality Check**
   - What were hidden costs (implementation, training, ongoing)?
   - Any surprise costs after contract signing?
   - Renewal pricing changes?

6. **Would You Choose This Again?**
   - Knowing what you know now, would you select this vendor?
   - What alternatives did you consider?
   - Any deal-breakers or show-stoppers?

**Document Findings:**
- Create simple summary table for each reference call
- Note patterns (multiple peers mention same issue = red flag)
- Ask for permission to follow up with technical staff if needed

---

### Step 4: Vendor Website & Documentation Review (1-2 hours)

**What to Evaluate:**

1. **Higher Ed Focus**
   - Does vendor have higher-ed-specific landing page?
   - Client list: How many higher-ed institutions? (vs K-12 or corporate)
   - Case studies: Are they higher-ed? (not just "education")

2. **Product Documentation**
   - Is documentation publicly accessible or behind paywall?
   - Quality: Screenshots, videos, API docs?
   - Higher-ed-specific features documented?

3. **Pricing Transparency**
   - Is pricing listed on website? (or "contact sales")
   - Higher-ed pricing vs. commercial?
   - Consortium or volume discounts available?

4. **Security & Compliance**
   - FERPA compliance statement?
   - SOC 2 Type II certification?
   - BAA (Business Associate Agreement) available?
   - Data residency options (US-based)?

5. **Integration & APIs**
   - Does it integrate with common higher-ed systems?
     - LMS: Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, D2L
     - SIS: Banner, PeopleSoft, Colleague, Workday
     - SSO: InCommon, SAML, LDAP
   - API documentation available?
   - Webhooks or event-driven integration?

6. **Support Model**
   - Business hours support or 24/7?
   - Higher-ed-specific support team?
   - User community or forum?
   - Response time SLAs?

**Red Flags:**
- No higher-ed clients listed
- No FERPA compliance mentioned
- No integration with common higher-ed systems
- Pricing completely opaque (could be inflated)
- Poor or missing documentation

---

### Step 5: Competitive Analysis (1-2 hours)

**Identify 2-3 alternatives:**

**Sources:**
- Educause vendor landscape reports
- Peer institution "we also evaluated..." mentions
- Internet2 NET+ alternatives in same category
- Gartner/Forrester higher-ed reports (if available)

**Comparison Matrix:**

| Criteria | Vendor A | Vendor B | Vendor C | Open Source Option |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Higher Ed Clients** | 150+ | 50+ | 300+ | Community-supported |
| **Educause Presence** | Strong | Moderate | Strong | Strong (consortia) |
| **NET+ Service** | Yes | No | No | N/A |
| **FERPA Compliant** | Yes (BAA) | Yes | Yes | Institutional control |
| **LMS Integration** | Canvas, Bb | Canvas only | All major | API available |
| **Pricing (estimated)** | $50K/year | $30K/year | $80K/year | Free (hosting costs) |
| **Support** | 24/7 | Business hours | 24/7 | Community + paid support |
| **Vendor Viability** | Established | Startup | Established | Mature project |

**Build vs. Buy vs. Open Source:**

| Option | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|--------|------|------|----------|
| **Buy (SaaS)** | Fast deployment, vendor support, regular updates | Recurring costs, vendor lock-in, less customization | Standard use cases, limited IT resources |
| **Open Source** | No license fees, full customization, community support | Hosting/maintenance costs, need technical expertise | Unique needs, strong IT team, budget-conscious |
| **Build Custom** | Perfect fit, institutional control, no vendor lock-in | High development costs, ongoing maintenance, technical debt | Truly unique requirements, strategic differentiator |

## Examples

### Example 1: Canvas LMS (Strong Higher Ed Fit)

**Educause Research:**
- 300+ case studies on Canvas migrations
- Active community group with 5,000+ members
- Annual conference: 50+ Canvas-related sessions

**Internet2:**
- Not NET+ service (direct vendor relationship)
- InCommon federation support (SSO)
- Large Internet2 member base using Canvas

**Peer Institutions:**
- Called 4 R1 universities: All positive
- Implementation: 6-12 months typical
- Support: "Best LMS support we've experienced"
- Would choose again: 4 of 4 yes

**Vendor Website:**
- 1,000+ higher-ed clients listed
- Extensive documentation and training resources
- FERPA-compliant BAA standard
- 24/7 support with higher-ed team

**Recommendation:** ✅ **Proceed** — Industry-leading higher-ed LMS with strong peer validation

---

### Example 2: Emerging AI Tutoring Platform (Proceed with Caution)

**Educause Research:**
- 2 brief mentions in AI landscape report
- 1 pilot case study from small liberal arts college
- No community group activity

**Internet2:**
- Not NET+ service
- No InCommon support (custom SSO integration required)

**Peer Institutions:**
- Found 3 institutions via vendor reference list
- Called 2: One positive, one "still evaluating after 6 months"
- Integration challenges with existing LMS
- Would choose again: 1 yes, 1 "too early to tell"

**Vendor Website:**
- 20 higher-ed clients (mostly small colleges)
- Limited documentation (sales-gated)
- FERPA compliance mentioned but no BAA details
- Startup (founded 2022, Series A funding)

**Competitive Landscape:**
- Alternative: Established vendor with 200+ higher-ed clients
- Cost: Similar pricing but more mature product

**Recommendation:** ⚠️ **Proceed with Caution** — Pilot with limited scope; require BAA; evaluate alternative in parallel

**Mitigations:**
1. Pilot with 1-2 courses before campus-wide
2. Require detailed BAA before processing student data
3. Budget for custom LMS integration (vendor support limited)
4. Set clear success criteria for pilot evaluation

---

### Example 3: Generic CRM (Evaluate Alternatives)

**Educause Research:**
- No mentions (vendor focuses on B2B sales, not higher ed)
- No case studies

**Internet2:**
- Not NET+ service
- No higher-ed community presence

**Peer Institutions:**
- Vendor provided reference list: All K-12 or corporate
- Found 1 higher-ed user via LinkedIn
- Called: "We're migrating away; doesn't fit academic workflows"

**Vendor Website:**
- Client list: 0 higher-ed institutions
- Pricing: Enterprise only (min $100K/year)
- No FERPA compliance mentioned
- No LMS or SIS integration

**Competitive Landscape:**
- Alternative: Salesforce Education Cloud (200+ higher-ed clients)
- Alternative: Open-source CiviCRM (used by 50+ universities)

**Recommendation:** ❌ **Evaluate Alternatives** — Poor higher-ed fit; better alternatives exist

**Rationale:** This vendor is optimized for B2B sales, not academic workflows. Lack of higher-ed clients, FERPA compliance, and academic system integrations make this a poor fit. Recommend evaluating Salesforce Education Cloud or CiviCRM instead.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: "Vendor Says They Do Higher Ed"
**Symptom:** Vendor claims higher-ed focus but has few institutional clients
**Consequence:** Mismatch between product and academic needs
**Fix:** Demand client list; independently verify with peer calls

### Pitfall 2: "Skipping Peer Research"
**Symptom:** Relying only on vendor-provided references
**Consequence:** Miss critical implementation challenges or hidden costs
**Fix:** Find peers independently via Educause, Internet2, LinkedIn

### Pitfall 3: "Assuming K-12 = Higher Ed"
**Symptom:** Vendor has K-12 clients; assumes higher-ed fit
**Consequence:** Product designed for different user model (parents, minors, simpler workflows)
**Fix:** Require higher-ed-specific case studies and references

### Pitfall 4: "Ignoring Open Source"
**Symptom:** Only evaluating commercial vendors
**Consequence:** Miss cost-effective, community-supported alternatives
**Fix:** Always include open-source option in competitive analysis

### Pitfall 5: "Rushed Research"
**Symptom:** Vendor evaluation completed in 1-2 days
**Consequence:** Miss red flags; regret during implementation
**Fix:** Budget 2-4 weeks for thorough research; don't rush procurement
